# Copyright Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-12.

Japanese: [著作権の規程（日本語訳）](./copyright.ja.md). It is a translation; where the two differ, the English text applies.

The Copyright Policy says that authors keep their copyright, and what Sophaxis copies and why.

The laws, licences and terms that this page cites are quoted at the end of each section that cites them.
Japanese statutes are quoted in full, as the e-Gov law API gives them. The other texts are quoted word for word from their official sources, as fetched on 2026-11-12, and are not translated.
A citation whose source could not be fetched is named there as not quoted, with the reason.

## Authors keep their copyright

Sophaxis asks for no transfer of copyright and no exclusive licence.
The work stays under the licence the authors gave it in the repository.

## What Sophaxis copies, and on what basis

Titles, author names, licences and dates come from the DOI metadata, which DataCite and Crossref make openly available for reuse; for Jxiv, they come from the public metadata of JaLC.
The abstract is part of the work.
It is shown under the work's own licence, with its authors and a link to the work.

Sophaxis keeps an unmodified copy of the PDF of each listed version, so that search engines can index the full text.
It copies the file by script only from a repository whose own pages let a script fetch it: Zenodo and arXiv.
Zenodo's developer documentation lists, among what its REST API supports, "Files — download/upload of files."
arXiv's page on bulk data access says "We ask that users intent on harvesting use the dedicated site export.arxiv.org for these purposes, which contains an up-to-date copy of the corpus and is specifically set aside for programmatic access." Sophaxis fetches from that site.
arXiv's terms of use for its APIs forbid storing and serving its e-prints "unless you have the permission of the copyright holder or are permitted to do so by the license with which the e-print was submitted". Every listed work carries one of the three accepted licences, which permit it.
Until 2026-11-12, Sophaxis also copied files from Figshare and Research Square by script. From 2026-11-13, it does not, because their terms forbid it.
Figshare's terms list, among what a user shall not do, "use any manual or automated means, including robots, scripts, or spiders to access, monitor, crawl, scrape, spider or mine". The only exception is for means "expressly authorized for you by Figshare in advance in writing".
Research Square's terms say "You also agree that you will not use any robot, spider, other automated device, or manual process to monitor or copy any content from the Web Sites".
The copies already taken from Figshare stay, under the work's own licence.
These pages were read on 2026-11-12: Zenodo's and arXiv's directly, and Figshare's and Research Square's from the Internet Archive's copies.
Where the repository publishes a checksum, the copy must match it.
When a deposit holds more than one PDF, Sophaxis does not guess which one is the paper, and makes no copy.
For any other repository, including Figshare and Research Square, the authors can attach the deposited PDF to their submission issue.
Sophaxis then keeps that file unmodified, and the record says that the authors provided it.
Without such a file, the record links to the repository only.
A copy is kept only if the file is at most 20 MB, and only while all the copies together stay within 1 GB.
A larger file stays at its repository, and the record links to it.

The copy is the file the authors deposited, shared under the work's own licence, with its authors, its licence and a link to the original.
The accepted licences allow this.
Authors can ask for the copy to be removed; the record then links to the repository only.

Each week, Sophaxis asks the Internet Archive to save its pages and the PDF copies, where every author of the work consents to it, so that they stay readable even if this site goes away.

Every record page carries the citation tags that Google Scholar reads.
Sophaxis does not control whether or when a search engine indexes a page.

## How Sophaxis meets the licences

Sophaxis shares a listed work only under the work's own licence, and only as that licence allows.
The three accepted licences permit copying and sharing for any purpose.
CC BY 4.0 and CC BY-SA 4.0 require credit; CC0 1.0 does not, but Sophaxis credits the authors anyway.

- Credit: every record page names the authors and the title, names the licence with a link to it, and links to the work by its DOI.
- No changes: the PDF copy is the deposited file, byte for byte, with every notice it carries. The abstract is shown as plain text, with formatting tags removed and its words unchanged.
- No added terms: Sophaxis adds no condition and no technical measure that would restrict what the licence allows readers to do.
- No endorsement: nothing on Sophaxis says or suggests that the authors endorse Sophaxis or its use of their work.

## Work published in a journal

When a journal publishes a work, the copyright or an exclusive licence may pass to the publisher.
The authors must then make sure that the version they deposited may be shared under its licence.
If it may not, the work cannot be listed.

## Material from others

Figures, tables, long quotations and other material from others need permission, or must be used in a way the law allows, such as quotation.
The authors are responsible for this.

## Examples of what is not allowed

These examples show how this policy applies. They are not a complete list.

The version that is shared:

- Depositing the publisher's typeset version under CC BY when the copyright went to the publisher. The authors can no longer license it.
- Depositing an accepted manuscript before the embargo that the publisher sets has ended.
- Attaching to the submission issue a PDF that differs from the deposited file, such as a newer draft, an edited copy or the journal layout. The copy must be the deposited file, unmodified.
- Asking Sophaxis to change its PDF copy. A change is a new version in the repository.

Material from others:

- A figure, a table, a map or a photograph copied from a book, a paper or a website without permission, beyond quotation.
- A long passage of another work, quoted at a length that the law of quotation does not cover.
- The full text of a poem, a song or a short story by someone else.
- A translation of someone else's paper, deposited as the authors' own work, or without the permission of the original's rights holder.
- Images from an archive whose terms forbid publishing them again.
- Material under a NonCommercial or NoDerivatives licence, placed inside a work that carries CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 or CC0 1.0 as though it were under that licence.
- A screenshot of software, a web page or a video, reproduced beyond what quotation allows.

Requests:

- Repeating the infringing material itself in a public removal issue. Describe where it is instead.
- Asking Sophaxis to remove a deposit from its repository. Sophaxis cannot; the request goes to the repository.

## Infringement

A rights holder who believes that a listed work infringes their rights can ask for its removal under the [Takedown Policy](./takedown.md).
