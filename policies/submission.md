# Submission Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-15.

Japanese: [投稿の規程（日本語訳）](./submission.ja.md). It is a translation; where the two differ, the English text applies.

The Submission Policy says who can submit what to Sophaxis, and what the submitter confirms.

The laws, licences and terms that this page cites are quoted at the end of each section that cites them.
Japanese statutes are quoted in full, as the e-Gov law API gives them. The other texts are quoted word for word from their official sources, as fetched on 2026-11-12, and are not translated.
A citation whose source could not be fetched is named there as not quoted, with the reason.

## Who can submit

If you are worried about your personal data because Vercel, which serves the site, or GitHub, which keeps the submissions, may use what they hold to train AI models, do not submit. The [Privacy Policy](./privacy.md) explains these uses.

Only an author of a work can submit it.
Sophaxis confirms this through ORCID, before a moderator reads the work.

- The ORCID record given in the form links to the GitHub account that opened the issue.
- The ORCID iD belongs to an author of the work: either the DOI metadata lists it for an author, or the ORCID record lists the DOI among its works and its name matches an author.

A submission that does not pass both is not listed.
From 2026-12-01, under the checked intake below, only the DOI metadata counts for the second check: it must list the ORCID iD for an author of the work.
The first check confirms that the person who controls the ORCID record opened the issue. It is not an official identity check.
Authors without an ORCID iD cannot submit.
From 2026-11-21, a person under 13 cannot submit.
A submitter under 18 needs the consent of a parent or guardian.
From 2026-11-22, Sophaxis does not list work by an author under 18 who lives or stays in India.
From 2027-05-13, Rule 10 of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 lets the data of a person under 18 be processed only with the verifiable consent of a parent, and Sophaxis does not verify that consent.
From 2026-11-22, Sophaxis does not list work by an author who lives in any of the 43 places in the table below, or who stays in one between the submission and the listing, and the submitter confirms this in the form.
For the 15 places for which the table gives 2026-11-26, this applies from 2026-11-26.
A stay counts even if it is short, such as for travel, work or study.
Stays count because the laws of several of these places reach people who are there, not only people who live there; Article 3(2) of the GDPR, for example, speaks of data subjects who are in the Union.
Sophaxis does not offer its service there, because it meets none of the duties that the law there places, or is reported to place, on a service offered to people there; the [Privacy Policy](./privacy.md) says more.
This rule does not depend on nationality, race, ethnic origin or any other personal characteristic, and Sophaxis never reads an author's nationality.
An author of any nationality who lives elsewhere and does not stay there can submit, and an author who lives or stays there cannot, whatever their nationality.
The reasons are laws that require a local representative, the storage of personal data in the country, or registration there (among them Article 27 of the GDPR and of the UK GDPR; Article 13 of the EU Digital Services Act; Article 53 of China's Personal Information Protection Law; section 37(5) of Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act), which Sophaxis, run unpaid by one person within free services, does not have; the operator has decided not to appoint a representative, as the Privacy Policy explains.
The rule is that Sophaxis is not offered where a law requires, or is reported to require, a service offered to people there to have a representative there, to keep personal data there, to register there, or to meet conditions before personal data leaves the place that Sophaxis cannot meet.
From 2026-11-26, the rule also covers two more kinds of duty that Sophaxis cannot meet.
One is a duty to give people there, before their personal data is collected, a postal address or a telephone number of the operator, which Sophaxis does not publish.
The other is a duty to process the personal data of a minor there only with a parent's consent that the service confirms, or not at all.
From the same day, a place is also not offered when its data protection law, found by a survey of the laws of 195 countries made on 2026-11-12, could not be read at its source, through its official address and one other route; the table gives such a place as "No, not fetched".
From 2026-11-29, a place is also not offered where the operator judges that offering Sophaxis would not conform to the law of that place.
When the operator so judges, the place and the reason are added to the table.
A place is added to the list as soon as such a duty is reported, before its law has been read at its source, and it is taken off only after that law has been read at its source and found not to reach Sophaxis.
Each change to the list is announced on the top page before it takes effect, as the Terms of Use set out.
Until 2026-11-30, Sophaxis relies on the submitter's confirmation and does not check where authors live or stay.
From 2026-12-01, Sophaxis also checks, for every author whose identity it verifies, that the public records the author set at least 30 days earlier place them where Sophaxis is offered.
The section "The checked intake" below says how, and its limits.
If it turns out that an author lives there, or stayed there between the submission and the listing, the record is removed under the [Takedown Policy](./takedown.md), and the operator reviews the decision on a representative.

## What can be submitted

Sophaxis accepts work in philosophy, mathematics, logic, the philosophy of mathematics and the philosophy of science.
Work that belongs mainly to another field is out of scope, even if it uses mathematics.

The work must already be deposited in Zenodo, Figshare, OSF Preprints, SocArXiv, MetaArXiv, arXiv, Preprints.org, SSRN, Research Square, SciELO Preprints, Qeios, ScienceOpen Preprints, Advance, KC Works or Jxiv, with a DOI.
It must carry CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 or CC0 1.0 in its DOI metadata, as set out in the [Licence Policy](./licences.md).
It must have a title, an abstract and references, in the ordinary form of a scholarly paper.

From 2026-11-23, a new work is listed only if at least six months have passed since its first DOI was registered.
The six months are counted to the day the submission issue is opened, in Japan time.
They end on the same day of the month, or on the last day of a shorter month.
The first DOI is the DOI registered earliest among the DOI given and the DOIs that its metadata names as earlier versions of the work, or as the DOI of all its versions.
In Zenodo this is the DOI of all versions, and in Figshare the DOI of the first version.
The day of registration is the one that DataCite or Crossref records.
Where the metadata gives no such day, as for Jxiv, the operator checks it by hand.
The automatic check in the issue reads these days and counts the six months.
A submission opened too early is not listed; a new issue can be opened once the six months have passed.
This does not apply to a new version of a work that Sophaxis already lists, or to a submission opened before 2026-11-23.

From 2026-11-23, a work for which Crossref records a retraction, a partial retraction, a withdrawal or a removal is not listed.
A work for which it records an expression of concern goes to the operator, who decides under the [Moderation Policy](./moderation.md).
Crossref includes the data of the Retraction Watch database.
The automatic check in the issue reads these notices for the DOI given.
A notice that a repository shows only on its own page, and not to Crossref, is not seen by the check.

Work that a journal has already published may be submitted only in a version that the authors may share under such a licence.
Publishers set different terms for this.
The submitter gives the DOI of the published version in the form, as set out under "When the work is published" below.

## When a work has more than one author

From 2026-11-21, a work with more than one author is listed only after every other author confirms their own agreement.
Each confirms in one of two ways.
One is a comment in the submission issue, from a GitHub account that their ORCID record links.
The comment says "I agree to the listing of this work on Sophaxis." and gives their ORCID iD.
The automatic check in the issue counts each confirmation.
The other is an email to the operator, after which the operator confirms them through their ORCID record.
This consent covers the listing, and the provision of their personal data that the [Privacy Policy](./privacy.md) sets out.
An email confirmation says the same, and the operator's reply links the Privacy Policy.
Saving the record to the Internet Archive needs a separate consent from every author, which the form asks and which may be left empty; without it the record is not saved there.
Any author can withdraw a consent at any time, by a removal issue or by email.
From 2026-12-01, under the checked intake, only the first way counts, and only for an author for whom the DOI metadata gives an ORCID iD.
An author for whom it gives none cannot confirm, and the work waits until the metadata at the repository names one.

## A researcher who vouches for a first submission

From 2026-11-23, a submitter who is not yet an author of a work that Sophaxis lists needs one researcher to vouch for it.
That researcher's public ORCID record lists a journal article, a book chapter or a book with a DOI, and links the GitHub account from which they comment.
The researcher comments in the submission issue with these words and their own ORCID iD: "I vouch for this submission to Sophaxis. I am not an author of this work, and I do not live in, or stay in, a place where Sophaxis is not offered."
The authors of the work and the operator cannot vouch.
Like an author, the researcher must not live in, or stay in, a place where Sophaxis is not offered.
Vouching says that the work is in scope and in the ordinary form of a scholarly paper.
It is not a review, and the moderator still decides.
The automatic check in the issue reads the researcher's public ORCID record as it reads a co-author's, and counts the vouching.
The researcher can take it back by deleting the comment before the listing.
A submitter who is already an author of a listed work needs no one to vouch, and neither does a new version of a listed work.

From 2026-12-01, for a submission whose issue is opened on or after that day, the researcher comments with these words instead, and the English words are canonical: "I vouch for this submission to Sophaxis. To my own knowledge, the submitter is an author of this work, holds the right to submit it under its licence, and does not live in, or stay in, a place where Sophaxis is not offered. I am not an author of this work, I am not from the same project as its authors, and I have no conflict of interest with them. I do not live in, or stay in, a place where Sophaxis is not offered. This states what I know; it is not a guarantee."
It is a statement of what the researcher knows, not a guarantee of what the submitter does.
Sophaxis makes no contract of guarantee with the researcher, and sets no amount.
If a vouching turns out to be untrue, Sophaxis no longer relies on it, and the listing is reviewed.
Before that is decided, the operator asks the researcher, in an issue comment that says nothing of what is said, to make contact by email to the address on the operator's ORCID record; the operator then tells them privately what is said to be untrue, and hears their answer. If no answer comes within 14 days, the review goes on.
No name, ORCID iD or account of a researcher is published as having vouched untruly, and Sophaxis keeps no list of researchers.
Sophaxis claims no money from the researcher under these policies.

## The checked intake

From 2026-12-01, for a submission whose issue is opened on or after that day, Sophaxis accepts a claim only when it can be checked against a source that the claimant does not write at the moment of claiming.
A claim that fails its check, or cannot be checked, is turned away automatically.
No system can stop a person from lying in general; this one makes every claim it accepts a checked claim.
Turning a claim away means a label and a fixed comment that do not accept the claim and say how to submit it again. The checked intake never closes or locks an issue; a person reads every one.

- Identity: the DOI metadata lists the ORCID iD for an author, and that ORCID record links the GitHub account that opened the issue.
- Where the authors live: the place rule below, for every author whose identity is verified, and for a researcher who vouches, who must also share no current organisation with the submitter in ORCID.
- The time: a submission that has not passed every check 30 days after its issue was opened is not accepted, with the label "not listed"; a new issue can be opened later.

The place rule reads, in the person's public ORCID record, only the country code (ISO 3166) of each public address and of the organisation of each current employment and education (one with no end date), with the day each was added. It reads no nationality, and nothing from GitHub.
A submission is not accepted if any entry names a place where Sophaxis is not offered, if the entries name more than one country, or if no entry names a place where Sophaxis is offered and was added at least 30 days before the issue was opened; a fixed comment then says how to make the record checkable.
Canada holds Quebec, so it places no one by its country alone.
The check keeps and writes none of the countries or organisations; the issue shows only how many people passed, never whose record or which entry.
This proves that earlier public records agree, not where a person lives: a person who keeps false records for 30 days can still pass.
The consequence of an untrue confirmation, and the removal of the record once the operator learns of it, therefore stay as the [Terms of Use](./terms.md#what-is-not-allowed) set them.
Nothing in the form asks the submitter to confirm what other people will do after the submission.

The checked intake never closes a removal or withdrawal issue, a request about personal data, a notice of an infringement of rights, a request about a statement about a person, or the withdrawal of a consent; a person handles these.

## How to submit

1. Deposit the preprint in Zenodo, Figshare, OSF Preprints, SocArXiv, MetaArXiv, arXiv, Preprints.org, SSRN, Research Square, SciELO Preprints, Qeios, ScienceOpen Preprints, Advance, KC Works or Jxiv, under an accepted licence, and obtain its DOI.
2. Link your ORCID record to your GitHub account: add `https://github.com/<your username>` under "Websites & social links".
3. Open a submission issue in the Sophaxis repository and give the DOI, your ORCID iD, the category and the statement on generative models.
4. An automatic check reads the DOI metadata and your ORCID record, and posts the result in the issue.
5. A moderator checks the submission under the [Moderation Policy](./moderation.md). If it passes, a record page is published.

The title, the authors, the abstract, the licence and the versions are taken from the DOI metadata, not typed in by hand.

The submission issue is public.
Your GitHub username and everything you write in the form will be visible to anyone.

## Language models and other generative models

Work written or developed with language models is accepted, on one condition: the use is disclosed.
The same condition applies to every other generative model, including models that produce images, figures, audio or code.

The disclosure must name the tools used and say what they were used for,
for example drafting, translation, proofs, code, literature search, editing, figures or images.
If no generative model was used, the statement says so.

From 2026-11-21, the form also asks for the extent of use, at one of four levels. The authors choose the highest level that applies:

- None: no generative model was used.
- Editing: language editing, translation or proofreading only.
- Assistance: structure, literature search or checking; the authors wrote the text.
- Drafting: a generative model drafted text, proofs, code or figures that the authors revised.

The record page shows the level the authors chose, above their statement. The level does not replace the statement; the statement still names the tools and what each did.
A level of "None" cannot stand beside a statement that names a model.

A language model cannot be listed as an author.
No other generative model can be listed as an author either.
The authors bear full responsibility for the work, as the [Terms of Use](./terms.md) set out.

If substantial undisclosed use comes to light after listing,
the record receives a notice, and the authors are asked to add a disclosure.

## Funding, competing interests, research ethics, and data

From 2026-11-24, the form asks three statements, and a work is listed only if each is given.
The automatic check in the issue confirms that each field is filled in; it does not judge what it says.

- Funding: every funder of the work, with grant numbers, or that the work had no funding.
- Competing interests: any financial or personal interest of an author that bears on the work, such as employment, consultancy, payments or a close relation to a person the work discusses, or that there is none.
- Human participants and research ethics: whether the work involves human participants, such as surveys, interviews or experiments with people.

Work that involves human participants must name the ethics committee that approved it, or say why no approval was needed under the rules that applied where the research was done.
Every participant who can be identified must have consented, and the statement says so.
Work that contains personal data of third parties is not accepted, as set out above.

The form also asks, as an option, where the data, code and other materials on which the work rests can be found, such as a repository with a DOI, or that there are none.
Sophaxis encourages authors to make them available, but does not require it and does not check them.

The record page shows each statement as the authors give it.
Sophaxis does not check the statements beyond reading them.
A statement found to be untrue is handled under "Research integrity" in the [Moderation Policy](./moderation.md).
This does not apply to a submission opened before 2026-11-24.

## Versions and updates

A work is listed once, under one Sophaxis id.
That id is never given to another work, even after a record is withdrawn or removed.

A new version is a new version in the repository.
Most repositories give each version its own DOI.
arXiv keeps one DOI for all versions, and that DOI leads to the latest one; the record then lists the same DOI with the date of each version.
Submit it as an update to the existing record; earlier versions stay listed.

## When the work is published

A listed preprint stays listed when a later version is published in a journal, in proceedings or in a book.
The record page then shows the published version at the top: what it is, where and when it appeared, and its DOI.
It asks readers to cite the published version, and it names the listed version as the preprint.

Any one author reports the published version with the [published-version form](https://github.com/cpsbvbng26-dotcom/sophaxis/issues/new?template=published.yml), from the GitHub account that the author's ORCID record links.
An automatic check confirms that the report comes from an author, and reads the metadata of the published DOI.
The published version must be a journal article, a conference paper, a book chapter or a book.
It must not be in one of the accepted repositories, and it must share an author with the record.
A moderator then records it, and the decision is public in the issue.
A published version can also be given at submission, and the same checks run on it.

Sophaxis does not judge the peer review of a journal.
The record repeats what the authors state: that the published version was peer-reviewed, that it was not, or nothing.
It also says whether the publisher's metadata links the published version to the preprint, or whether only an author reported it.

Each week Sophaxis looks in the metadata of Crossref and DataCite for a published version that is linked to a listed preprint and shares an author with it.
It records nothing by itself: it asks the authors to report it.
The same weekly check confirms that a recorded published version still resolves.
When the publisher adds a retraction, a correction or another notice to the published version, the record page shows it.

Some publishers ask authors to remove or change a preprint on publication.
The authors check the publisher's terms.
If the listing must go, any one author can withdraw it under the [Withdrawal Policy](./withdrawal.md), and the record page keeps the link to the published version.

## Examples of what is not accepted

These examples show how the rules above apply. They are not a complete list; the rules decide.
Each group matches a reason that a moderator names when declining, under the [Moderation Policy](./moderation.md).

Authorship: the submitter must be an author, and every author must agree.

- Submitting a colleague's paper for them, even with their permission. Only an author can submit.
- Submitting a paper by a teacher, a supervisor or a famous author because it deserves to be read.
- Opening the issue from a GitHub account that the ORCID record does not link to, such as a lab account or a second personal account.
- Giving the ORCID iD of a co-author, or of anyone other than the person who opens the issue.
- Giving an ORCID iD that neither the DOI metadata nor the ORCID record connects to the work.
- Submitting a work when a co-author has not agreed, or has said no.
- Opening a new GitHub account to submit a declined work again under another name.

Repository and DOI: the work must have its own DOI from an accepted repository.

- A PDF on a personal website, a university page, Google Drive, Dropbox, Academia.edu or ResearchGate.
- A work on PhilArchive, PhilSci-Archive or HAL alone, which give no DOI of their own. Deposit it also in an accepted repository.
- The DOI of the version a journal published, given as the DOI of the deposit.
- A different work given as the published version of a listed preprint.
- The DOI of an article in a SciELO journal, rather than in SciELO Preprints.
- The DOI of a peer review report, as Qeios and ScienceOpen also register.
- A DOI that does not resolve, or that leads to a different work.

Licence: the DOI metadata must carry CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 or CC0 1.0.

- CC BY-NC 4.0, CC BY-ND 4.0, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 or CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.
- CC BY 3.0, CC BY 2.0 or any other earlier version of a Creative Commons licence.
- The arXiv non-exclusive licence (arXiv.org perpetual, non-exclusive license).
- "All rights reserved", or no licence in the DOI metadata at all.
- A licence written only in the PDF or on the repository page, but not in the DOI metadata.
- A licence changed after listing to one that is not accepted. The weekly check reports it, and the operator looks into it.

Scope: the work must belong mainly to philosophy, mathematics, logic, the philosophy of mathematics or the philosophy of science.

- A paper in physics, economics, computer science, engineering, biology or psychology that uses mathematics as a tool.
- A machine-learning benchmark, a software description or a data set.
- Literary criticism, history or political commentary that does not argue a philosophical or mathematical question.
- Self-help or spiritual guidance that gives advice or practice instead of arguing a philosophical question, or a political manifesto or campaign.

Form: the work must have a title, an abstract and references, in the ordinary form of a scholarly paper.

- Slides, a poster, lecture notes or a recorded talk.
- A blog post or an essay without references.
- An abstract alone, or a title page without the full text.
- Several unrelated papers in one deposit.
- Fiction, poetry or a diary.

Disclosure: the statement on generative models must name the tools and say what they were used for.

- "None" when a language model drafted, translated or edited any part of the text.
- A statement that says only that a language model was used, without naming the tool or saying what it did.
- Figures or images made with a generative model that the statement leaves out.
- A language model or another generative model listed as an author in the DOI metadata or in the paper.

Rights or personal data: the authors must be free to share everything in the work, and it must not expose others.

- A figure, a table or a photograph copied from a book, a paper or a website without permission, beyond what quotation allows. Citing the source does not replace permission.
- The publisher's typeset version of a journal article, when the publisher allows only the authors' manuscript to be shared.
- Names, email addresses, postal addresses or photographs of private persons who have not agreed.
- Survey or interview answers from which a participant can be identified.
- Screenshots of private messages, emails or chats.
- Unpublished material of another researcher, shared without their consent.

Spam or abuse.

- The same work submitted again and again, or under several titles.
- Many submissions opened at once to flood the issues.
- Advertising, links to paid services, or text written to sell something.
- Text that harasses, threatens or defames a named person.
- Attempts to break the form, the automatic check or the site, for example by placing code or markup in the fields.
- Pretending to be another person, an institution or a journal.

Versions.

- A new version submitted as a new work, instead of as an update to the existing Sophaxis id.
- A new version of a withdrawn record. A withdrawn record takes no new versions.

## Withdrawal

Any one author can withdraw a listed work under the [Withdrawal Policy](./withdrawal.md).
Records are not deleted: a withdrawn work keeps its record page with a withdrawal notice, so that citations to it do not break.
An author can instead have the record removed, so that only its Sophaxis id stays.
Removal for legal reasons follows the [Takedown Policy](./takedown.md).
