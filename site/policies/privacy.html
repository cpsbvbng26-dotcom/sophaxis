Who handles personal data

The operator of Sophaxis, Takuya Nemoto, handles it. The operator's address is not published; it is given on request, without delay. Sophaxis applies Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information in full, as if its operator were a business operator under that Act.

Purposes

Personal data is used only for these purposes:

  • to confirm that a person who submits, withdraws or reports a published version, or a co-author who confirms their agreement, is an author of the work;
  • from 2026-11-23, to confirm that a researcher who vouches for a first submission is not an author of the work and has a journal article, a book chapter or a book;
  • to publish record pages, author pages, the lists and the search;
  • to offer the records for reuse in feed.xml, papers.json and the OAI-PMH interface at /oai;
  • to check the listed DOIs and licences each week, and, for works whose authors consent to it, to save the pages and the PDF copies to the Internet Archive each week and when they change;
  • to tell search engines which addresses are new, changed or removed;
  • to answer requests to withdraw, correct or remove a record, and requests about personal data;
  • to serve the site, and to find and fix faults from Vercel's request logs;
  • to answer questions, complaints, disputes and security reports.

The site

The Sophaxis site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other servers. Its pages contain no code or tag that sends information about a reader to anyone else. The rules of Japan's Telecommunications Business Act on sending users' information outside (Article 27-12) bind only a telecommunications carrier and a person who runs a telecommunications business that the Act exempts from registration and notification, and among those only the services that the Ministry's Ordinance names; they do not cover information needed to use the service. Sophaxis charges for nothing and earns nothing from the site, so the operator considers that it runs no telecommunications business and that Article 27-12 does not apply to it. The Ministry's Ordinance, fetched on 2026-11-12, names among the services these rules reach one that sends information at the request of unspecified users for them to read; it does not settle whether a service that earns nothing is a business that is run. Sophaxis therefore meets these rules as if they applied, and gives here, for each request that its pages make a reader's browser send, the three things that the Ordinance asks to be made known. The only such request is the search page's request to Sophaxis's own site for its list of records (papers.json). What is sent: what any browser sends with a request, such as the IP address, the name of the browser and the address asked for. Who handles it: the operator of Sophaxis, named on the Operator and contact page, and Vercel Inc., which hosts the site. What for: to give the search page the list that it searches. The Ordinance leaves out of these rules the information truly needed to provide the service, and the operator considers that this request is of that kind. The search page runs one script, served by Sophaxis itself. It reads the list of records from Sophaxis and searches it in your browser, and it sends nothing anywhere. No other page runs a script.

Each record page has share links to X, Bluesky, Facebook, LinkedIn and email. They are plain links to each service's own page. Nothing is sent anywhere until a reader follows one, and the service then works under its own privacy policy.

The site is hosted on Vercel. Vercel processes each request, including the IP address, in order to serve the page, under its own privacy policy. Sophaxis has not turned on Vercel's analytics. No page, a record page included, shows a count of views or downloads, and Sophaxis keeps no such count: it runs no analytics that would make one, and it does not count readers from Vercel's request logs.

Submissions

Submissions are public GitHub issues and are subject to GitHub's terms. Your GitHub username and everything you write in the form become public.

When a submission issue is opened or edited, a GitHub Action reads two kinds of public data. It reads the metadata of the DOI from DataCite, Crossref or JaLC (Japan Link Center). It reads the name, the websites and the works of the ORCID record given in the form. It uses them only for the checks, and writes nothing else from the ORCID record anywhere. The same check runs when a withdrawal issue or a published-version issue is opened or edited. The check asks ORCID only for the personal details (the name), the websites and the works sections of the record. It does not ask for the person section as a whole, so public email addresses and the country are never received. The check uses only the name, the websites and the works, and keeps or writes nothing else from the ORCID record. The automatic comment in a submission issue repeats the draft record, which is public already. From 2026-11-21, a co-author who confirms their agreement in a comment gives their ORCID iD there, and the check reads their public ORCID record in the same way. From 2026-11-23, a researcher who vouches for a first submission in a comment gives their ORCID iD there, and the check reads the name, the websites and the works of their public ORCID record in the same way. Their GitHub username, ORCID iD and comment are public in the issue, by their own act.

Giving an ORCID iD, and opening the issue from a GitHub account that the ORCID record links, are needed to submit, withdraw or report a published version. Without them the check cannot confirm the author, and the request cannot go ahead by issue; it can still go by email, as each policy says. Nothing else is asked of anyone.

Records

A record holds the title, the names and ORCID iDs of the authors, the abstract, the licence, the DOIs and dates of the versions, and the statement on generative models. Most of a record comes from the metadata of the deposit. The statement on generative models, an abstract given in the form, a reason for withdrawal and notes on corrections come from the issues, where they are public already. A record also gives the number of its submission issue, which shows the submitter's GitHub username. Sophaxis keeps records without a time limit, so that citations do not break. The issues, with the GitHub usernames in them, stay as long as GitHub keeps them; removing a record empties the issues that name it, and the operator then deletes them. Vercel keeps its request logs for the period its service sets, and the operator does not copy them. A removed record keeps only its Sophaxis id and the day of removal, as the Takedown Policy sets out. Earlier versions stay in the history of the public repository and in the saves of the Internet Archive; the operator asks GitHub and the Internet Archive to remove them.

Reuse and copies elsewhere

The records are offered for reuse in feed.xml, papers.json and the OAI-PMH interface at /oai. Harvesters, such as OpenAIRE, copy the title, the authors, the abstract and the licence from there. The OAI-PMH interface gives the same email address as the operator's ORCID record, because the protocol requires one. Requests about personal data go to the address on the operator's ORCID record, as below. The pages and the PDF copies of a work whose authors consent to it are saved to the Internet Archive. Works deposited at Zenodo can also be gathered in the Sophaxis community at Zenodo, https://zenodo.org/communities/sophaxis. An author submits the record there, and Zenodo, run by CERN in Switzerland, holds it. Sophaxis copies nothing from the community, and the operator does not change the records in it. A request to add the record of a work by an author who lives in a place where Sophaxis is not offered, as the Submission Policy lists them, or who stayed in one between the submission and the listing, is declined from the date the Submission Policy gives for each place (2026-11-22, or 2026-11-26 for 15 of them), because such a work is not listed on Sophaxis; the request itself stays with Zenodo, under CERN's own terms. When a record changes, its address is sent to search engines through IndexNow; only the address is sent. Copies that others have already taken are outside Sophaxis's control.

Services outside Japan

Sophaxis uses these services outside Japan: GitHub (United States) to receive submissions and keep the records, Vercel (United States) to serve the site, DataCite (Germany) and Crossref (United States) for DOI metadata, ORCID (United States) for public ORCID records, and the Internet Archive (United States) to save the pages and the PDF copies. OpenAlex (United States) is asked how it holds a listed work, by its DOI only, never about a person. Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI (all United States) receive, while a submission is reviewed, its public parts (the issue, the DOI metadata and the abstract), which the operator gives by hand to their language models, Claude, ChatGPT and Grok, to help the review; nothing is given to them after a work is listed. Sophaxis treats the personal data in records that reaches GitHub, Vercel, ORCID and the Internet Archive, and the public parts of a submission that reach Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI while it is reviewed, as provided to a third party in a foreign country, under Article 28 of Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information. The submission form asks for each author's consent to this, after giving the information below; the confirmation is required from 2026-11-21. Until then, the operator lists a work by an author other than the operator only after every author has given this consent in the submission issue. Consent to the Internet Archive is asked in a box of its own, which may be left empty; a record without it is not saved there, and robots.txt asks the Internet Archive not to collect it. Any author can withdraw either consent at any time, by a removal issue or by email, and what was done before the withdrawal stays lawful. Withdrawing the consent to the Internet Archive stops the saves without review, once the operator confirms that the request comes from an author; the operator then asks the Internet Archive to exclude the earlier copies. Withdrawing the consent to GitHub, Vercel and ORCID removes the record, because Sophaxis cannot list a work without them.

Two uses of the records by GitHub and Vercel themselves come with their terms, which Sophaxis cannot change:

  • GitHub's Terms of Service let GitHub and its affiliates use what is posted in issues and public repositories, the records among it, to train AI features and the models of its affiliates (section D.4). There is no setting that turns this off for public content (section J.3).
  • Vercel's Terms of Service let Vercel, on the free Hobby plan on which Sophaxis is served, use the content of the site, the records among it, to train AI and machine learning models, and share it with third parties for that purpose, unless the account turns this off in its team settings (section 3). The operator has not confirmed that this setting is turned off for the Sophaxis account.

If these uses make you worry about your personal data, do not submit, and do not agree to be listed as an author, because Sophaxis cannot list a work without GitHub and Vercel. An author of a listed work can withdraw the consent at any time, and the record is then removed, as set out above.

Requests sent by email reach the operator's mailbox at Apple (iCloud Mail, United States), and are kept there only as long as needed to answer them and to show the answer. For users in Japan, where the operator lives, the iCloud Terms of Service name the Apple company that provides iCloud as iTunes K.K., located at Roppongi Hills, 6-10-1 Roppongi, Minato-ku, Tokyo 106-6140. The iCloud Terms let Apple access, use, preserve and disclose the content of an account, for example to comply with the law, to enforce the terms, or to deal with security, fraud or technical issues, so iCloud handles the personal data in the emails. As the Personal Information Protection Commission's Q&A 7-53 reads Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information, keeping them there is therefore a provision of personal data, and Sophaxis treats iTunes K.K. as entrusted with that handling (Article 27(5)(i)) and supervises it under Article 25 of the Act. iTunes K.K. is a company in Japan, and the Commission's guidelines on provision to a third party in a foreign country decide who is a third party by its legal personality, so the mailbox is not a provision to a third party in a foreign country under Article 28 of Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information, and no consent is asked of the people who write. Apple says that personal data may be processed by Apple Inc. and other Apple companies on behalf of the Apple company that controls it, and Apple Inc. is in the United States. As the Commission's Q&A 10-24 asks where an entrusted company has the data handled abroad, Sophaxis takes the system of the United States, set out above, into account in the measures it takes, and says so under "Security". What Apple says about its own measures is in the Apple Privacy Policy: its privacy practices comply with the Global Cross-Border Privacy Rules (CBPR) System and the Global Privacy Recognition for Processors (PRP) System, and BBB National Programs, its accountability agent, lists Apple Inc. as a current participant in both. A person in the European Union who writes reaches the operator in Japan, for which the European Commission has made an adequacy decision, and the mailbox at Apple in the United States; Apple says that its international transfers of personal data collected in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland are governed by Standard Contractual Clauses.

Vercel keeps request logs for Sophaxis, including the IP address. The operator can see them, and uses them only to find and fix faults. Vercel's documentation says that, on the Hobby plan on which Sophaxis is served, runtime logs are kept for 1 hour. A request log holds details of the request, such as an IP address, the browser's user agent, the address asked for and the time, and no name. Sophaxis holds nothing that links an IP address to a person: it has no accounts, sets no cookies and runs no analytics, and finding who used an IP address would need an inquiry to that person's network provider. Under Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information, information is personal information when it identifies a person, alone or because it can easily be matched with other information; the Commission's General Guidelines read matching that needs an inquiry to another business as generally not easy. The request logs are therefore not personal information for Sophaxis, and Article 28 of Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information, which concerns personal data, does not apply to them. Vercel also receives each request directly from the reader's browser, and Sophaxis gives Vercel nothing from the logs. If a person gives their own IP address to ask about their requests, the operator treats the matching entries as that person's personal information while answering, and copies none of them. Vercel handles the logs as its Privacy Notice sets out, and publishes the measures it takes in Schedule 2 of its Data Processing Addendum. Each week, an automatic step reads the date of the last update of Vercel's and Apple's privacy statements; a new date opens an issue, and the operator reads the statement again and decides whether the service can still be used.

The services outside Japan may not be required to protect personal data in a way that, overall, provides safeguards comparable to those of the law of the place where an author lives, such as New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020. Section 22 of New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020, in its information privacy principle 12, lets personal information be disclosed abroad on the person's authorisation after the person is told this expressly, and the submission form says it next to the consent. For a person in Switzerland, the disclosure to the services in the United States rests on that person's explicit consent, which Article 17 of Switzerland's Federal Act on Data Protection allows.

Security

Records are built only from information that is public already. The automatic checks keep nothing from an ORCID record except the name, the websites and the works. The automatic checks run with the least permissions they need. Only the operator can change the records, except that the automatic step of the Takedown Policy takes the record page (its title, authors, abstract and PDF copy) offline when a removal issue reports exposure of personal data. If personal data leaks in a way that the law requires to be reported, the operator reports it to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission and tells the people concerned. A leak must be reported when it involves sensitive personal information, data whose misuse could cause financial loss, data leaked through an act done for an improper purpose, such as an attack on the operator's accounts, or the data of more than 1,000 people. The operator reports promptly after learning of it, and gives the full report within 30 days, or within 60 days when the leak may come from an act done for an improper purpose.

The measures, under the headings that Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission uses:

  • Organisation: one person, the operator, handles personal data and answers for it. Every change to the records is a public commit or pull request.
  • People: no one else handles personal data for Sophaxis. Before a moderator is invited, the operator writes here what personal data a moderator may handle and how the operator supervises it.
  • Physical: Sophaxis has no servers or papers of its own. The records live in the GitHub repository and the site on Vercel.
  • Technical: the automatic steps hold no key but the token GitHub gives each run, with the least permissions they need. Earlier versions of the site on Vercel are not open to the public. The operator's GitHub account, the only one that can change the repository, uses two-factor authentication. The operator signs in to Vercel, which serves the site, through that GitHub account.
  • The countries where data is kept: as set out under "Services outside Japan" above. The emails in the operator's mailbox may be handled by Apple Inc. in the United States on behalf of iTunes K.K.; the operator keeps them only as long as needed, and takes the system of the United States into account, as set out there.

Automated processing

The automatic checks compare public records and post the result in the issue. They make no decision about a person. No decision with legal or similarly significant effects on a person is made solely by automated means. The language models that help the review make no decision; every decision on a submission is taken by a person, as the Moderation Policy sets out. One step acts before the operator decides: when a removal issue reports exposure of personal data, it takes the record page (its title, authors, abstract and PDF copy) offline. It protects the person concerned, and is undone if the request is not upheld. Every automatic step is listed on the page What runs by itself.

Your choices

An author can withdraw a work, have its record removed so that only the Sophaxis id stays, or ask for the PDF copy to be removed. A person whose personal data appears in a record can ask for its removal under the Takedown Policy. A person can ask for access to, correction of, suspension of the use of, or deletion of their personal data held by Sophaxis, through a removal issue that names only the Sophaxis id and the kind of request, or by email to the address on the operator's ORCID record. A person can also ask to be told the purposes for which their data is used, to have its use restricted while its accuracy or an objection is checked, and to have its provision to third parties stopped. While a restriction lasts, the operator takes the part concerned offline. The submission issue, with its consent, the history of the record in the public repository and the file docs/records-of-provision.md, which names each recipient with its address, are the record of provision to third parties (Article 29 of Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information); a person can ask to see what concerns them. The records are kept for at least three years, as the APPI Enforcement Rules require. A person whose data is not in a record, such as a GitHub username in an issue or an IP address in Vercel's logs, writes by email. Before answering a request about personal data, the operator confirms who is asking, through the ORCID record or by email. A request can also be made through an agent, such as a parent or guardian or a person the requester appoints; the operator then confirms the agent's authority as well as the person's identity. A copy of personal data is sent by email, in a commonly used electronic form, or by another method the person asks for, and never in an issue. The operator aims to answer within 7 days. A request about personal data is answered within one month at the latest. If a request needs more time, the operator says so within that month, with the reasons, and answers within three months of the request. When the operator does not act on a request about personal data, it says why within one month. It also says that the person can complain to a data protection authority and can go to court. On request, the operator tells a person which recipients were told of a correction, a removal or a restriction of their data. Requests cost nothing. A complaint about how personal data is handled goes to the same email address. A person can also consult Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission. A person in Switzerland can also complain to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.

These rights are open to everyone, wherever they live. Where a person's own law gives them more rights over their personal data, such as the General Data Protection Regulation in the European Union, the operator honours those rights as far as they apply to Sophaxis. Personal data that GitHub or Vercel hold about their own users, such as GitHub accounts, is theirs to handle under their own privacy statements; a request about it goes to them.

The right to object

Anyone can object, at any time, to the use of their personal data on Sophaxis, on grounds of their own situation. An objection goes by a removal issue or by email. After an objection, the operator stops the use objected to, unless it shows compelling legitimate grounds that override the person's interests, rights and freedoms, or the data is needed for legal claims. The operator gives those grounds in writing.
