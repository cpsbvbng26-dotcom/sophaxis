# The policies compared with other services

Last updated: 2026-11-21.

On 2026-11-21 the owner asked for the policies and terms of Sophaxis to be compared with those of arXiv, SSRN and other services, and improved where they fall short.
This page records what was read, what was taken over, and what was not, with the reason for each.
It is a working record. It says nothing for or against any of these services, and none of them has any connection with Sophaxis.

## What was read

- arXiv: its help pages, from the public repository in which arXiv keeps them (`arXiv/arxiv-docs` on GitHub, commit `a536f556` of 2026-11-10), read in full on 2026-11-21. The pages read were those on moderation, on appeals, on content types, on the code of conduct and its enforcement, on the submittal agreement, on identity and affiliation, on replacing and withdrawing an article, and on journal references.
- EarthArXiv, OSF and Zenodo: texts that each keeps in a public repository or record. These were EarthArXiv's moderation policy (version 2.9) and FAQ, the Center for Open Science's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, and Zenodo's Terms of Use (version 1.2), with the configuration of its software.
- SSRN, bioRxiv, medRxiv, PhilArchive, Preprints.org, Research Square, HAL, ChemRxiv, Jxiv, SciELO Preprints and Qeios: their own sites cannot be reached from the machine that wrote this page. What they say was seen only as short quotations in search results, not on the pages themselves. Nothing here relies on those quotations alone.

## What was taken over

Each change is in the feed of policy changes under 2026-11-21, and check 145 holds it.

| Point | Where it was seen | What Sophaxis now says | From |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A decision can be appealed only by an author. The appeal must give a reason. Repeated requests with nothing new are not considered. | arXiv, "Appealing a moderation decision" | The submitter or another author answers within 30 days of the decision, with a reason or something new. A request by anyone else is not taken up, and an answer that only repeats is not looked at again (Moderation Policy, "Decisions"). | 2026-12-06 |
| A declined work can be submitted again. | Not from any one service; added for clarity beside the rule above | A declined work can be submitted again, in a new issue, once the reason for declining no longer applies (Moderation Policy, "Decisions"). | Publication |
| Moderators may reclassify a submission. | arXiv, "Reclassification of works" | A moderator may list a work under another category or subfield that fits it better. The moderator says so in the issue, with the reason, before the listing (Moderation Policy, "Decisions"). | 2026-12-06 |
| Proposals for future research, calls for papers and news are not accepted. | arXiv, "Policies for specific content types" | A work must report research of its own. A research proposal, a call for papers, an announcement or news is not accepted (Submission Policy, "What can be submitted"; Moderation Policy, the reason "Not in the form of a scholarly paper"). | 2026-12-06 |
| Work is disseminated without regard to personal characteristics. | arXiv, "Code of Conduct" | A decision does not take into account the nationality, race, ethnic origin, religion or belief, sex or gender, sexual orientation or disability of any author. Where the authors live or stay counts only as the Submission Policy sets out (Moderation Policy, "Decisions"). | Publication |
| A replacement should say what changed. | arXiv, "To replace an article"; Jxiv and ChemRxiv in search results | The submission form asks, optionally, what changed in a new version, in at most 300 characters. The record page shows it beside the version (Submission Policy, "Versions and updates"). | Publication |
| A paper is not withdrawn because it is updated or published. | arXiv, "Withdrawing an article" | A withdrawal is not needed to show a new version or a publication (Withdrawal Policy, "How to ask"). A withdrawal stays the authors' right, because every author's agreement is a condition of the listing. | Publication |
| An author's name can be changed in the metadata of every version. | arXiv, "Identity and Affiliation"; EarthArXiv | An author whose name has changed can ask, by email, for the record's pages to show the current name in every version, with no reason and no proof asked. The unmodified PDF copies keep the name the PDF carries, and the author can ask for them to be removed (Withdrawal Policy, "An author's name"). | Publication |

## What was not taken over

| Point | Where it was seen | Why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A fixed number of new submissions per month. | arXiv | From 2026-12-10, Sophaxis already allows no new work while the submitter, or an author whom the DOI metadata names by ORCID iD, is an author of a work listed in the 180 days before (Submission Policy, "Higher barriers"). That is stricter. |
| A promise of when an appeal is decided. | arXiv ("most appeals are resolved within a two week period") | The Moderation Policy gives decision times only as a guide, with no fixed number of days, and check 22 holds that. One person, unpaid, cannot promise a number of days. |
| Images likely to cause offence may be declined. | arXiv | The fields of Sophaxis rarely carry such images. A ground that turns on offence would ask the moderator to judge taste, and the rules on rights, personal data and abuse already cover what harms a person. |
| Work generated mostly by a language model is not accepted. | OSF and Zenodo, in search results | Sophaxis accepts any use that is disclosed at one of four levels and names no model as an author. A reader can judge from the disclosure. This is a commitment of the policies and is not weakened. |
| Undergraduate research, theses and course projects are not accepted. | arXiv, bioRxiv, EarthArXiv, SSRN | Sophaxis decides on the work, not on the author's position or stage of study. A thesis or a student's paper that reports research of its own and meets the other rules is accepted. |
| Screening for harm to the public, health claims and dual-use research. | bioRxiv, medRxiv, ChemRxiv, Research Square | These belong to the life sciences and chemistry, which are outside the scope of Sophaxis. |
| Software that compares the text with other works. | arXiv, bioRxiv, Jxiv | The policies say that Sophaxis runs no plagiarism software. Plagiarism is a reason for declining, and a concern can be raised in public. |
| A waiver of moral rights and an indemnity from authors. | arXiv's submittal agreement | The Terms of Use rest on Japanese law. They limit liability only as that law allows, and from 2026-12-01 ask for compensation only for an untrue confirmation. A wider waiver would be one-sided toward authors. |

## Where Sophaxis already does the same or more

The comparison found these already in the policies, in some cases beyond what the services read here say:

- A withdrawn record that stays, with its reason.
- Earlier versions that stay listed.
- The link to a published version.
- No language model as an author, and a statement on its use.
- Statements on funding, competing interests and research ethics.
- A reason named for every decline.
- Removal only on grounds that the Takedown Policy names (rights, personal data, the law, or where an author lives or stays) or at an author's request.
- At least 14 days' notice before a change that narrows a right or adds a duty. OSF and Zenodo say that changes take effect when posted.
- An accessibility statement.
- A named governing law.
- Copies at the Internet Archive.
